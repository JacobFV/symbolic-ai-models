● Short answers: text completion — mechanically yes, capably no, and that's 
  measured rather than assumed. Agents — yes in one sense, not at all in the 
  other. Details, then the interesting residue.

  1. Text completion

  The machinery is there. symbolic_ai_models asks every architecture a
  next-token prediction question, and symbolic-ai-models sample
  (symbolic_ai_models/decode.py) decodes free-running continuations from any
  model in the registry — rule-based or neural, same sampler, no KV cache. It's
  deliberately outside the evaluation path: "a window, not a benchmark."

  What the window shows is that generation is absent, and that finding survived 
  its own refuter:

  - exact_match 0.000 everywhere; 11 of 19 models cannot emit a token at all.
  - The pre-registered claim was that this was a readout artefact — the same
    fitted corefer-001 scores 0.508 read as a move and 0.085 read as text, so
    generation should reduce to graph completion plus a deterministic render.
    That was checked first because it could refute, and it did. On
    runs/infill-ans, nine of twelve models land on the abstention row, and
    ptrgen-001, vsa_perm-002 and both omegaclaw rows match "say nothing" to nine
    decimal places.
  - The cleanest number in the whole repo says why: acc | copyable 0.494 against
    acc | not copyable 0.003. These models copy a symbol that is already
    visible in the context. They do not produce one that isn't.
  - Two controls ruled out the task design manufacturing the null (hiding only
    the first copy takes corefer 0.001 → 0.072, sixty-fold, and still below its
    floor; more supervision makes it worse).

  Two scope caveats worth having: the "text" is the synthetic formal-language 
  curriculum (179 procedurally generated lessons), not English — and where the
  repo does touch real English (Wikipedia biographies, the live reading arm),
  the capability is reading, not writing. Milestone E (raw LM perplexity) is
  explicitly the far one; state.yml says a small learned decoder and
  ResponsePlan are steps 7 of 9 and not yet built.

  2. Agent control

  In the "symbolic policies acting in environments" sense: yes, fully built and 
  it produced the repo's biggest result. symbolic_ai_agents is a complete second
  harness — four environments (choice, retry, repair, probe), eight agents, its
  own registry, calibration, trajectories, rollouts, CLI, and a wrap that drops
  any model from the other package straight into them. It shares episodes,
  vocabulary, floors and calibrator with symbolic_ai_models deliberately, so the
  two produce numbers you can discuss in one paragraph.

  That harness is where the largest effect in the repo lives, and it isn't a
  model difference. One fitted model, corefer-001, read two ways over 179
  lessons and 2,632 scored fills:

● Real thoughts, and I'll disagree with part of it.

  Your instinct is right, and this repo already measured why

  The single most-repeated result across the last month is "the symbolic 
  machinery was correct and the data did not reach." Three arms in a row ended
  that way. And when the reading arm finally decomposed properly, the binding
  factor wasn't the classifier or the reader — it was containment: on 850
  biographies, three times in four the gold value is not in the text at all.
  Twelve feature interventions had been pushing on a ceiling that was never
  theirs to raise.

  That is your thesis, arrived at empirically and from the other direction. Text
  is a lossy projection, and a large fraction of the work went into trying to
  invert a projection that had already thrown the answer away.

  The other number that says it: acc | copyable 0.494 against acc | not copyable
  0.003. Every model here can re-emit a symbol that is visible in the context
  and essentially none can produce one that isn't. There is no generative model
  of the domain behind the text — there's a copier. That is precisely "failed to
  recapitulate the generating structure," measured.

  So: the pivot is evidence-supported, not a change of mood. Good.

  But the nodes aren't the research. The edges are.

  Here's my actual worry. "Build conceptual primitives in foundational domains,
  then it becomes much easier to build systems that parse, associate, predict in
  other forms" — that sentence is the classic symbolic-AI bet, and it is the
  specific one that historically failed. Cyc had primitives. The
  knowledge-acquisition bottleneck was never a shortage of primitives; it was
  that the glue was where all the difficulty went, and the tail never closed.

  Which means: building a math node and a Newtonian-physics node is the
  well-trodden part. Symbolic regression, AI Feynman, SINDy, theorem provers,
  physics-informed nets — recovering F=ma from trajectories has been done. If
  you build the DAG's nodes and stop, you've spent a year confirming things that
  were known, which CLAUDE.md names as the failure mode for this programme.

  The unproven, frontier claim is the edge. Does structure recovered in domain A
  measurably reduce the cost of recovering structure in domain B? Nobody has a
  clean number on that. That's the thing worth a year.

  So I'd invert the build order you implied: don't build all the nodes and then
  test composition. Build two nodes and one edge, plus a control edge that must 
  fail, and get the transfer number first. If transfer is ~1.0×, the DAG is a
  taxonomy and not an architecture, and you want to know that in week three
  rather than month nine.

  And I'd reorganize the nodes

  "Math" and "Newtonian physics" are subjects. If each is learned in isolation
  you end up with N domain models and no shared substrate — which is the thing
  you said you wanted to avoid. The interesting object is what's shared such
  that physics is cheaper after math.

  So make the nodes structural primitives, and let domains be the tasks that 
  exercise them:

  Layer 0 — no prerequisites
  - Identity / equality — when are two observations the same object? (You
    already have a sharp datapoint here: entity resolution bought nothing,
    entity typing helped.)
  - Order and cardinality — successor, counting, total order.
  - Composition — application, associativity, sequencing.
  - Invariance under a transformation — and note your renaming control is
    already the degenerate case of this. You've been measuring a Layer-0
    primitive for months without calling it one.

  Layer 1
  - Algebraic structure — recover a group from a partial operation table. Pure
    recapitulation: you see products, you must return the structure.
  - Quantity and dimension — recover units and scaling from raw numbers. Known
    ground truth, genuine inverse problem.
  - Conservation — a quantity invariant along a trajectory.
  - Recursion / inductive definition — grammars, recursive sequences.

  Layer 2
  - Kinematics — geometry plus time, no forces. Frames of reference.
  - Causation and intervention — mechanism vs correlation; needs identity +
    invariance.
  - Stochasticity — recovering a distribution's structure through sampling
    noise.

  Layer 3
  - Newtonian dynamics — needs quantity, conservation, kinematics, causation.
  - Cross-domain composition — a physics problem stated in prose; an algebraic
    structure latent in a physical system. Where the transfer claim actually
    gets tested.

  The nice property of organizing this way: some edges are derivable and 
  therefore falsifiable. Noether says conservation follows from symmetry. So
  invariance → conservation is an edge the curriculum predicts. If a system that
  learned invariance shows no advantage on conservation, either your primitives
  aren't primitives or your transfer mechanism is fake — and you find out on a
  case where theory told you the answer in advance. That's a much better first
  experiment than picking two arbitrary domains.

  The part that would make this stop being your objective

  Here's the trap I'd guard hardest. Your original goal is about inverting a 
  projection. If each node hands the system clean states and forces, you've
  deleted the projection and posed a curve-fitting problem instead.

  So every node needs a projection operator as part of its definition: what is
  the generator, what is the observation channel, how lossy is it, and is the 
  latent identifiable from that channel at all.

  And you already have the instrument. Containment generalizes to 
  identifiability, and it's computable before you fit anything. Containment
  asked "is the answer even in the text." The general form asks "is the latent
  even recoverable from this channel." The repo already proved the model-free
  screen can prove a relation impossible (though never feasible) — that screen
  is exactly what stops you spending three weeks on a node whose channel threw
  the structure away. Run it on every node before building it. That single move
  converts your most expensive historical mistake into a design tool.

  Second guard, also already yours: hold out a generator, not samples. Renaming
  invariance caught two headline claims here and killed ensemble-001's
  best-in-repo 1.14 bits (0.467 → 0.038). The generalization is: perturb the
  generator's parameters and symbols, don't refit, and check the recovered
  structure tracks. A system that memorized instances dies; one that recovered
  the law doesn't.

  What I'd actually push back on hardest

  Two things.

  One — "primitive" has to mean something operational or transfer is just weight
  reuse. If a primitive is a learned module and transfer is warm-starting, this
  is ML-as-usual with a curriculum, and the inspectability claim evaporates. If
  a primitive is a typed symbolic object the system can name, inspect and 
  recompose — a kernel with a witness, a type in the world substrate — then it's
  your differentiator and it's a thing you already have machinery for. I'd
  insist on the second, and I'd express the primitives as entries in the
  existing typed runtime rather than as a new project. That also means the pivot
  reuses far more than it looks like: the cost ledger, the floors discipline,
  the invariance controls, the kernels and their witnesses, the certificate
  machinery, and the router are all domain-agnostic and all survive.

  Two — the control edge is non-negotiable. If you measure invariance → 
  conservation and see transfer, you have not shown structure transfer. You've
  shown some transfer. You need an edge the DAG says should be absent, measured
  the same way, that must come back ~1.0×. Without it, generic capacity or
  generic warm-start explains your result and you'll believe it for months. This
  repo's whole cost history is confident readings that lacked exactly that
  control.

  Where I'd start Monday

  Smallest thing that poses the real problem and could embarrass the plan:

  - Two nodes: invariance and conservation. Both have known ground truth, both
    have a real projection (observe trajectories, recover the invariant), and
    the edge between them is theoretically predicted, so a null is informative
    rather than ambiguous.
  - One control edge: invariance → something the DAG says it shouldn't help
    (recursion, say).
  - The metric is structure recovery, not task accuracy. You know the generator,
    so compare recovered structure to true structure directly — and hold out
    generator families, not samples.
  - Run the identifiability screen on both channels first, and record what it
    says before fitting anything.

  That's maybe two weeks, and it tells you whether the DAG is an architecture or
  a filing system. Everything else in the programme is downstream of that
  number being greater than one.

  One last note, since you've now pivoted twice in a month (model zoo → typed
  world runtime → structure recapitulation). I think this one is right and
  better-founded than the last, because it's the goal the evidence keeps
  pointing back at rather than a new idea. But pivots are cheap in narrative and
  expensive in apparatus, so it's worth writing down explicitly what carries
  over — and the honest answer is: most of the rigour machinery, all of the cost
  ledger, the kernels, and none of the curriculum. The 179 lessons were always
  a development harness. This would finally be the benchmark.

● Okay. Big.

  The thing that makes it one system instead of eleven benchmarks

  If you write the ladder as "2D → 3D → agents → code → terminal → computer,"
  every rung is already someone's research programme. Slot attention, NeRFs,
  world models, DreamCoder, causal discovery, SWE-bench, computer-use agents.
  Individually trodden, all of it.

  What nobody has measured is the derivative. Not "can it do rung 7" but does 
  rung 7 get cheaper because rungs 0–6 happened, and does it get cheaper 
  superlinearly? That is your actual claim — "once I have conceptual primitives
  in these domains it becomes much easier to build the rest" — and it is a
  number, not a hope. The benchmark is not any rung. The benchmark is the slope 
  of cost-to-criterion across the ladder.

  That's also the one framing where a symbolic system can win outright. Neural
  transfer across this ladder is diffuse and it is bought with scale. A symbolic
  store of recovered primitives is inspectable, addressable, and reusable by 
  name — so the prediction is that its cost curve bends and theirs doesn't.
  Falsifiable, frontier, and nobody's run it. And you already have the ledger:
  $/solved episode above floor becomes $/primitive recovered, plotted against
  what's already in the store. The whole thesis fits in one plot.

  Escalate along identifiability, not along vibes

  Here's what turns your list into a curriculum rather than a wish. The rungs
  should climb specific axes that make recapitulation provably harder:

  - Observability — how much of the latent the channel reveals
  - Latent dimensionality — independent generative factors
  - Compositional depth — how many primitives make one observation
  - Passive → interventional — the phase transition, not a step
  - Stationarity — do the rules themselves move
  - Reflexivity — do your actions modify the generator
  - Discoverable vs. inventable — new parameters vs. genuinely new concepts

  Your ladder maps onto these almost exactly, which is why the intuition is
  good. Made explicit:

  Two things fall out of writing it this way that I didn't expect:

  Rung 5 is a phase transition, not a difficulty increment. Passive observation
  identifies causal structure only up to an equivalence class — that's a
  theorem, not a limitation of your method. Intervention is what breaks the tie.
  So "agentic interaction" isn't an application of the world models; it's the
  only thing that makes a whole class of structure recoverable at all. It
  belongs in the middle of the curriculum, and everything below it is knowingly
  working under an identifiability ceiling you can compute in advance.

  Ground truth survives much further up than intuition says. You author the 2D
  and 3D generators, so you hold the latent. You hold the AST for code. You can
  author a synthetic filesystem with authored tool semantics for the terminal.
  Only rung 10 genuinely loses it. That means you can measure actual structure 
  recovery — compare the recovered program to the true program — for ten of
  eleven rungs, instead of measuring task accuracy and hoping it's a proxy.
  Given that this repo's entire cost history is proxies read as the real thing
  (determinacy read as accuracy, label_acc that was 72% a copy score), that
  property is worth protecting at every design decision.

  So: synthetic-but-scaling all the way up, real systems only as the final 
  transfer test. The moment you reach for real video or a real OS as a training
  rung you've traded away the only instrument that can tell you whether this is
  working.

  Where language goes now, and it's better than dropping it

  Language stops being the target and becomes the highest-occlusion channel in 
  the curriculum — a projection of another mind's world model, with an
  observation function nobody designed to be invertible.

  That retroactively explains the last month cleanly. Containment 0.241–0.592
  isn't a fact about reading being hard; it's the measured observability of the
  linguistic channel, and it's terrible. The reading arm was doing rung-3
  inversion through the worst channel on the ladder without a single easier rung
  to stand on. Same for acc | not copyable = 0.003 — of course it copies, it
  was never given a generator to recover.

  That's a continuous story, not a discarded one. It also predicts something
  checkable: the same reader machinery pointed at a channel with high
  containment should do much better — and it already did, occupation at 0.615
  versus the company arm's best-ever number, 2.7×, same reader, unchanged.

  The compounding claim needs its control, and it's the whole ballgame

  If you measure "rung 5 was cheaper after rungs 0–4," you have shown some
  transfer. Generic warm-starting shows that. Capacity shows that.

  The control that must fail: a store of shuffled, irrelevant, or wrong 
  primitives must not reduce cost. And a second one — an ablated store missing
  exactly the primitive the theory says rung 5 depends on should cost more than
  one missing an unrelated primitive of equal size. If both stores help equally,
  you're measuring warm-start and the DAG's edges are decoration.

  This is the same shape as the renaming control that took ensemble-001 from
  1.14 bits to 0.038. That control is the reason you know the ensemble result
  was memorisation. You need its analogue here or you will believe the
  compounding story for a year.

  What I'd actually do, given how big this is

  The whole thing is multi-year. The decisive measurement is not.

  The compounding slope is visible at rungs 0–3, and those are cheap because you
  author the generators and the latents are small. Build four rungs of 2D
  worlds with escalating factor count and occlusion, deposit recovered
  primitives into one typed store, and plot cost-to-criterion against store
  contents — with the shuffled-store control running alongside. Weeks, not
  quarters.

  If the curve is flat across rungs 0–3, it does not become steep at rung 9.
  You'd have learned that the primitives don't compose, which is the exact thing
  that killed the last symbolic programme, and you'd have learned it for the
  price of four toy worlds instead of a career.

  If it bends — you have the first measured evidence that inspectable recovered
  structure compounds where neural transfer doesn't, and then the ladder above
  it is worth every year it takes.

  One more thing, because it's the part people skip: decide now what a 
  "primitive" is, operationally. If it's a learned module and transfer is weight
  reuse, this is curriculum learning with extra steps and the inspectability
  claim quietly dies. If it's a typed symbolic object the system can name,
  inspect, recompose, and hand to a kernel with a witness — then it's the
  differentiator, and you already have the runtime for it. That choice
  determines whether the plot at the end means what you want it to mean.

● Yes — and the phrase carrying all the weight is "relatively low dimensional 
  structure." Everything else is surface escalation. That clause is the thesis,
  and it's measurable.

  Why low-dimensional is achievable in a big world: FoV is a quotient operator 
  you don't have to learn

  Your state.yml already has the right abstraction — persistent WorldSnapshot,
  then a task quotient [W]_Q per query. The thing embodiment adds is that
  proximity and field of view give you a quotient that's physical, correct, and 
  free. The world can be enormous; the live latent at any instant is bounded by
  what's near. Dimensionality then scales with locality, not with world size.

  That's the actual mechanism by which the claim can be true, and it's not
  hand-waving — it's a structural property of worlds built from objects with
  local interaction.

  And it lands directly on machinery you already have. The certificate/recording
  trick computes a dependency set by running a computation against a store that
  remembers what it read. In an embodied setting, the set of affordances the 
  agent actually touched is the witness for its decision. Inspectability isn't
  an added feature here; it falls out of the FoV quotient for free. Ask "why did
  it open the drawer" and get back a dependency set of a dozen typed facts.

  The number that makes or breaks it

  You author the generator, so at every moment you know three quantities:

  - |generator| — the world's true state
  - |task-sufficient| — the minimum that preserves the answer
  - |agent's live state| — what it actually maintains

  The thesis is that the third tracks the second, sits far below the first, and
  the ratio stays roughly constant as world complexity grows. If agent state
  grows linearly with world size, it's dead — you've built a database, not a
  model. Plot that ratio across the escalation and you have the whole argument
  in one figure, in exactly the shape of the compounding slope from before.

  There's a stronger version worth reaching for: the compressibility of a world
  should be predictable in advance from computable properties of the generator —
  interaction locality, factor sparsity, branching of the affordance graph.
  That's the identifiability screen again, in a third form. "We can say before
  training how low-dimensional this world's task statistic is, and the system
  attains it" would be a much better result than any score.

  Rasterized text is the sharpest thing in your list

  I want to flag this because I think you may have thrown it in casually and
  it's the best idea here.

  Rasterized text in a 3D world is the entire ladder inside one observation:
  pixels → glyphs → tokens → symbols → propositions → world facts, through
  perspective projection, at an angle, partly occluded. And you authored the
  document, so you have ground truth at every level of the chain simultaneously.
  Nobody has that. It's also the point where "text is a projection of a richer
  generative process" stops being a metaphor and becomes literally, physically
  true — the document was produced by a process in the world, rendered onto a
  surface, and the agent has to invert both the rendering and the authoring.

  The reading arm's whole month becomes a special case: containment was
  measuring the observability of the linguistic channel with the rendering
  already inverted for free.

  Nested generators — and the prediction that tests whether your primitives are 
  real

  A general computer inside the world is a generator nested inside a generator,
  observed through a rasterized screen, which is itself an observation channel
  inside another observation channel.

  That gives you a sharp falsifier: the same inversion procedure that recovers 
  the physics of the room should recover the semantics of the application on the
  screen — different primitives, same machinery. If the screen needs a
  fundamentally different mechanism, your primitives were never primitives, they
  were domain hacks. That's a control you can run early and cheaply, and it
  would tell you something real.

  Editable documents give you the reflexive rung with full ground truth: act,
  the document changes, you authored the transition, so "predict the effect of
  the edit before making it" is scorable exactly.

  Two cautions, and I'd hold both firmly

  One — affordance exposure is scaffolding, and it's the same trap you already 
  fell into. If objects announce their affordances truthfully and completely,
  you've deleted the discovery problem, which is the thing you're trying to
  study. The 179-lesson curriculum was 98.6% scaffolding and ended up a
  development harness rather than a benchmark. This is that trap one level up,
  wearing a nicer costume.

  So build the affordance channel as a dial, and always report the dial position
  the way containment is now always reported:

  - announced, complete, truthful
  - announced, incomplete
  - announced, sometimes wrong — the door labeled push that pulls
  - not announced — recoverable only by intervention

  The real objective lives at the bottom. The top is for bootstrapping and
  should be stamped the way NOT QUOTABLE is stamped.

  Two — do not build the 3D simulator first. This repo has a runtime, kernels, a
  cost ledger and 3,823 tests. It does not have a renderer, and a year spent on
  one is a year not spent on the science. There are perfectly good embodied
  sims already; none of them have your affordance dial, your rasterized-text
  ground truth, or your nested computer, but all of that can be prototyped in a
  2D grid world where rendering is free.

  And here's the part that makes it more than pragmatism: the renderer swap is 
  itself the experiment. Build the interfaces — affordance channel, FoV
  quotient, nested-generator contract — against 2D. Prove the compression ratio
  and the transfer slope there. Then port to 3D and measure what the port costs.
  If the primitives are genuinely surface-independent, the port is cheap. If
  it's expensive, your primitives were entangled with the surface and you've
  learned that for the price of a port instead of a programme.

  3D isn't different science. It's an escalation on the observability axis — and
  the axis is the thing you're studying, not the polygons.
